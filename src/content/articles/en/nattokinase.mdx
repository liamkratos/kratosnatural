## Introduction: What is Nattokinase?

**Nattokinase** is a fibrinolytic serine protease enzyme extracted from *natto*, a traditional Japanese food made from fermented soybeans using *Bacillus subtilis* fermentation. In recent years, scientific research has focused intensely on nattokinase's potential role in cardiovascular health.

As of 2026, 338 peer-reviewed studies on nattokinase are indexed in PubMed. That figure and its breakdown by study type come from our own literature search rather than from any single cited source. The six studies cited below are the ones this analysis draws its numbers from.

This analysis synthesizes evidence from these studies to provide a picture of:

- Mechanism of action (fibrinolysis)
- Optimal dosing protocols (with evidence of dose-response)
- Clinical efficacy for blood pressure, atherosclerosis, and cardiovascular markers
- Safety profile, as reported by the cited studies
- Comparison to pharmaceutical alternatives (statins)

## How Nattokinase Works: The Fibrinolytic Mechanism

Nattokinase's primary mechanism is **fibrinolysis** — the breakdown of fibrin, a protein responsible for blood clot formation.

### Fibrin Degradation

Nattokinase dissolves clots by directly hydrolyzing fibrin. A review of its mechanism reports that it also converts endogenous prourokinase to urokinase, degrades plasminogen activator inhibitor (PAI-1) and raises tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA) levels <Cite n="weng2017" />. That review does not compare nattokinase's fibrinolytic activity against pharmaceutical thrombolytics.

### Anti-Atherosclerotic Effects (Independent of Lipids)

Nattokinase reduces atherosclerotic plaques without primarily working through cholesterol reduction. The mechanism appears to involve fibrin degradation within plaque deposits, reduced inflammation in arterial walls, and improved endothelial function. In a direct comparison against simvastatin in 82 patients, both reduced plaques, but nattokinase was more effective (36.6% vs 11.5% reduction), despite not lowering cholesterol as much <Cite n="ren2017" />.

### Blood Pressure Regulation

Proposed mechanisms for nattokinase's blood-pressure-lowering effect include decreased renin activity (angiotensin pathway inhibition), improved vascular elasticity, and reduced arterial stiffness.

## Dosing: The Dose-Response Relationship

Dosage matters considerably for nattokinase. In the largest study, 3,600 FU/day produced no effect on plaque or lipids while 10,800 FU/day did, and the lower-dose subgroup of the meta-analysis showed unfavourable lipid changes. This is a case where "more is not always better," but "too little shows no benefit."

| Dosage | Study | Participants | Duration | Primary Results | Efficacy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10,800 FU/day | Chen et al. (2022) | 1,062 total cohort (subgroup size not reported) | 12 months | CIMT and carotid plaque size reduced; improvement rates 66.5-95.4%; lipid profile improved | Effective <Cite n="chen2022" /> |
| 6,000 FU/day | Ren et al. (2017) | 82 | 26 weeks | Carotid plaque ↓ 36.6%; outperformed statin | Effective <Cite n="ren2017" /> |
| 2,000 FU/day | Kim et al. (2008) | 86 (73 completed) | 8 weeks | Systolic BP ↓ 5.55 mmHg; diastolic BP ↓ 2.84 mmHg | Effective <Cite n="kim2008" /> |
| 3,600 FU/day | Chen et al. (2022), same cohort | Subgroup size not reported | 12 months | No effect on lipids or plaque | Ineffective <Cite n="chen2022" /> |
| Lower-dose subgroup (cut-off not reported) | Li et al. (2023) meta-analysis | 546 (6 RCTs) | Not reported | Total cholesterol MD +5.27; HDL MD -2.76 (units not stated in abstract) | Associated with worse lipids <Cite n="li2023" /> |

### A Non-Linear Dose-Response Curve

Nattokinase appears to show a threshold effect rather than a linear dose-response curve: at 3,600 FU/day no benefit was observed, while 6,000 and 10,800 FU/day produced measurable effects on plaque. Doses above 10,800 FU/day were not tested in the studies cited here, so nothing is claimed about them. The exact threshold between these points has not been established by the studies reviewed.

## Blood Pressure: Clinical Evidence & Effect Size

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 6 randomized controlled trials (546 participants total) found systolic blood pressure reduced by a mean difference of -3.45 mmHg (95% CI: -4.37 to -2.18, p&lt;0.00001) and diastolic blood pressure by -2.32 mmHg (95% CI: -2.72 to -1.92, p&lt;0.00001) <Cite n="li2023" />. The individual study durations are not reported in that abstract.

In chronic hypertension research generally, each 1 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure is associated with a meaningful reduction in cardiovascular risk, so a change of this magnitude over 8-12 weeks is considered clinically relevant, particularly alongside other lifestyle interventions.

### Individual Trial: Pre-Hypertension

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 86 participants (73 completed), ages 20-80, with pre-hypertension or Stage 1 hypertension (SBP 130-159 mmHg), tested nattokinase 2,000 FU/capsule vs. placebo over 8 weeks. Net change in systolic BP was -5.55 mmHg (95% CI: -10.5 to -0.57, p&lt;0.05); net change in diastolic BP was -2.84 mmHg (95% CI: -5.33 to -0.33, p&lt;0.05); renin activity fell by -1.17 ng/mL/h vs. control (p&lt;0.05) <Cite n="kim2008" />. That abstract reports no safety or tolerability data, so no claim about adverse effects is made from it.

## Atherosclerosis & Plaque Reduction: The Strongest Evidence

This is nattokinase's most substantial clinical evidence, with larger effect sizes than the blood-pressure data.

### Landmark Study: 1,062 Participants

A clinical study of 1,062 participants (the largest nattokinase study cited here) over 12 months examined carotid intima-media thickness (CIMT), plaque size, and lipid profile <Cite n="chen2022" />.

- **10,800 FU/day group:** significant reduction in both CIMT and carotid plaque size, with improvement rates of 66.5-95.4%; improved lipid profile; no noticeable adverse effects recorded.
- **3,600 FU/day group:** no reduction in carotid plaque, no improvement in lipids.

Additional findings from this study: co-administration of Vitamin K2 and aspirin appeared to enhance results; effects appeared stronger in subgroups including smokers, alcohol drinkers, and higher-BMI individuals; regular exercise appeared to further improve outcomes. The authors' interpretation is that the cardiovascular benefit is independent of cholesterol, working primarily through fibrin degradation in arterial plaques.

### Head-to-Head: Nattokinase vs. Statin

A randomized comparison trial in 82 patients (39 nattokinase, 37 statin; 76 completed) over 26 weeks compared nattokinase 6,000 FU/day against simvastatin 20 mg/day <Cite n="ren2017" />:

| Measure | Nattokinase | Statin |
|---|---|---|
| Carotid plaque size reduction | 36.6% | 11.5% |
| CIMT reduction | Reported pooled across both groups: 1.13 → 1.01 mm (p&lt;0.01); per-group values not reported | Included in the pooled figure |
| HDL cholesterol | Increased | No change |
| Total cholesterol | Decreased | Decreased more |

Nattokinase produced greater plaque reduction despite a smaller cholesterol-lowering effect, consistent with a mechanism distinct from statins.

## Cholesterol & Lipids: Why Nattokinase Isn't a Statin

Nattokinase is not primarily a cholesterol-lowering agent, and research shows dose-dependent, sometimes counterintuitive lipid effects.

The Li et al. (2023) meta-analysis (6 RCTs, 546 participants) found <Cite n="li2023" />:

**Lower-dose subgroup:** total cholesterol MD +5.27 (95% CI: 3.74 to 6.81, p&lt;0.00001), HDL MD -2.76 (95% CI: -3.88 to -1.64, p&lt;0.00001), LDL MD +6.49 (95% CI: 0.83 to 12.15, p=0.02) — all directionally unfavorable. The abstract does not state the units for these mean differences, nor the FU cut-off separating the subgroups.

**Higher-dose subgroup:** total cholesterol MD +3.18 (95% CI: 2.29 to 4.06, p&lt;0.00001, still slightly unfavorable), no significant difference in HDL or LDL, and no significant correlation with triglycerides (p=0.71).

This suggests nattokinase's cardiovascular benefit is independent of cholesterol modification, and it should not be expected to lower cholesterol the way a statin does. For people managing both high cholesterol and atherosclerotic disease, a statin and nattokinase address different mechanisms; always consult a healthcare provider before combining supplements with medications.

## Safety & Adverse Effects

Three of the six studies cited here report safety data, and none of them recorded serious adverse effects at the doses studied, up to 10,800 FU/day. The remaining sources do not report safety outcomes, so they neither support nor contradict this.

| Study | Participants | Duration | Max Dosage | Adverse Effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chen et al. (2022) | 1,062 | 52 weeks | 10,800 FU/day | None reported <Cite n="chen2022" /> |
| Gallelli et al. (2021) | 153 | Variable follow-up | 100 mg/day | None; no drug interactions reported <Cite n="gallelli2021" /> |
| Li et al. (2023) meta-analysis | 546 (6 studies) | Not reported | Varied | No notable adverse events <Cite n="li2023" /> |
| Kim et al. (2008) | 86 | 8 weeks | 2,000 FU/day | Abstract reports no safety data <Cite n="kim2008" /> |

Proposed reasons for this safety profile include protease specificity (nattokinase primarily cleaves fibrin rather than other critical proteins), breakdown in stomach acid limiting systemic toxicity, a short half-life limiting bioaccumulation, and a long history of human consumption via fermented food.

### Cautions

- **Bleeding risk:** nattokinase may theoretically thin the blood; use caution alongside anticoagulants and monitor INR with a healthcare provider.
- **Natto allergy:** exceedingly rare, but anyone allergic to fermented soy should avoid nattokinase supplements.
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** safety data is insufficient; consult a healthcare provider before use.

## Nattokinase vs. Statins: A Mechanistic Comparison

| Factor | Statin (e.g., simvastatin) | Nattokinase |
|---|---|---|
| Primary mechanism | Blocks cholesterol synthesis (HMG-CoA reductase inhibition) | Fibrin degradation (fibrinolysis) |
| Effect on cholesterol | Lowers LDL (magnitude not sourced here) | No significant effect; slight increase in total cholesterol in both dose subgroups of the meta-analysis |
| Effect on plaques | 11.5% carotid plaque reduction over 26 weeks in the head-to-head trial | 36.6% carotid plaque reduction over 26 weeks in the same trial |
| Plaque reduction (direct comparison) | 11.5% (Ren et al. 2017) | 36.6% (Ren et al. 2017) |
| Time to effect | Not sourced here | Plaque change measured at 26 weeks and at 12 months in the studies cited |
| Reported adverse effects | Not sourced here | None recorded in the three cited studies that report safety data |

These are not necessarily mutually exclusive interventions, since they work through different mechanisms. Someone whose primary issue is high cholesterol has decades of statin evidence to draw on; someone whose primary concern is atherosclerotic plaque may find the plaque-reduction data above relevant. Anyone considering combining the two should do so under medical supervision.

## Practical Guidelines by Health Goal

**For blood pressure:** 2,000 FU/day, the dose at which blood pressure fell over an 8-week trial. That trial did not run beyond 8 weeks, so it says nothing about longer-term maintenance <Cite n="kim2008" />.

**For atherosclerosis and plaque reduction:** 10,800 FU/day was the dose used in the largest study showing 66-95% plaque reduction over 12 months; that study also noted apparent synergy with Vitamin K2 and aspirin, and with regular exercise <Cite n="chen2022" />.

**For general cardiovascular health:** 100 mg/day was the dose studied in a 153-patient vascular-disease population. That study reports the dose in milligrams and gives no FU equivalent, so it cannot be compared directly with the FU doses above <Cite n="gallelli2021" />.

**Points worth keeping in mind:**
- 3,600 FU/day showed no benefit in the largest study, and the lower-dose subgroup of the meta-analysis showed worse lipid markers.
- Nattokinase should not be expected to lower cholesterol — that mechanism belongs to statins, not nattokinase.
- Effects on plaque and vascular structure take months, not days, to appear.
- Anyone on anticoagulants should discuss nattokinase with a physician and monitor relevant bloodwork.
