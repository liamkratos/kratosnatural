## 1. What €1B actually requires

Exposing the arithmetic so nobody has to take the number on faith.

**Direct-to-consumer only:**

- €60 average order value × 6 orders/year = €360 per active customer per year
- €1B ÷ €360 = **2,8 million active repeat customers**
- Netherlands population: 17,9M → that is **15,6% of every man, woman and child** buying six times a year

Not reachable. Not with better marketing, not with any marketing.

**Therefore both of these are mandatory, not optional:**

1. **Geography.** NL+BE is 29M people. NL+BE+DACH+UK+Nordics is ~200M. At 200M
   the same €1B needs 1,4% penetration. Hard, but a real number.
2. **Retail distribution.** Supermarket shelves multiply reach without paying
   for each customer. Upfront's step change came the year Albert Heijn and
   Jumbo listed them (2023: €6,3M, tripled).

And one that follows from both:

3. **Category expansion beyond supplements.** A salt lamp and nattokinase do
   not reach €1B in any geography. Upfront's ladder — dominate one category,
   take adjacent shelves, then move into normal groceries — is the only shape
   that gets there. Ours has to end in food too.

**The revenue curve, with years:**

| Year | Target | Multiple | What makes it true |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | €250K | — | First product live, audience started |
| 2027 | €2,5M | 10× | Product range + coaching + guides, NL |
| 2028 | €12M | 4,8× | Retail listing NL, BE launch |
| 2029 | €45M | 3,8× | Full range in NL retail, DACH launch |
| 2030 | €120M | 2,7× | Own production, food categories begin |
| 2032 | €350M | — | DACH+UK at scale |
| 2035 | **€1B** | — | Food is the majority of revenue |

Shape check against Upfront: founded 2020, €6,3M in 2023, €70M in 2025. Five
years to €70M. Ours is nine years to €1B, from a harder start but with a
stronger moat. Aggressive. Not fantasy.

## 2. The three brands: decide this now

The biggest risk in this whole plan is running three brands at 33% attention.
Upfront's most expensive lesson was a clothing line that ate "a ton and eight
months," and their stated takeaway was *focus is the most important thing for
building something large*. We already have the clothing line. It is called
Krealio.

**The decision:**

- **Kratos Natural is the €1B vehicle.** Everything in sections 3–8 is about
  this. It is the only one of the three with a category big enough.

  It also carries one constraint the others do not: **everything sold here
  traces to a published source.** That is the moat, and it is why faith guides
  are not sold here — not because they are worth less, but because a shelf
  that cannot meet the standard, standing beside shelves that do, teaches a
  sceptic to doubt both.
- **Liam Kratos is the person, and the apex offer.** A person is more
  followable than a company, and a person is also allowed to hold convictions
  a research brand is not. So this is where the faith guides live, where the
  essays live, and where coaching lives.

  **Coaching is the whole library, delivered by hand.** Not a longevity
  protocol — the integration of everything the guides cover, for one person at
  a time. That is a defensible thing to charge properly for, because the
  alternative on offer is reading a hundred and sixty guides and working out
  the order yourself.

  It still does not scale, and must never be allowed to consume the week. But
  it stops being a side proof of credibility and becomes the top of the ladder:
  free research → paid guide → the whole thing, with a person.
- **Krealio is a hobby with a shop attached, and that is fine.** It gets
  built once, cheaply, and then left alone. It does not get a content engine,
  a launch calendar, or a percentage of attention. Revisit in 2030 or never.

If that ordering is wrong, stop reading — the rest of the plan is downstream
of it.

**Immediate hygiene:** Krealio's `messages/nl.json` still carries Kratos
Natural's copy (`stat1` talks about systematic reviews and meta-analyses on an
art brand). Fix or delete before it ships.

## 3. The content engine

This is the whole plan. Everything else is logistics.

### The principle we are copying

Upfront never led with the product. Their arc, verified from their own archive:

1. **2023 — the customer's problem.** Pure running how-to. 8,8K views. No brand.
2. **2024 — the industry's dishonesty.** Honey, olive oil, matcha, iced tea.
3. **2025 — their own numbers, including the bad ones.** Margins, losses, failures.
4. **2026 — human stories and spectacle.** An 80-year-old's last marathon.

Product launches ride on top of an audience that came for something else.

### Our version, and why ours is stronger

They publish their margins. **We publish the studies that found nothing.**
That is a harder, rarer, more defensible act of honesty, and it is currently
the quietest thing on our site. It should be the loudest.

Concretely: the salt lamp page already says *"Wat wij niet claimen: in de
grootste meta-analyse over cognitie haalden apparaten voor thuisgebruik geen
significantie."* That sentence is the entire brand. It should be a video, not
a footnote.

### The four content lines, in launch order

**Line 1 — Service (start now, never stop).**
Answer the question the customer already typed into Google. Not our products,
their problem. Upfront's 2023 titles were search queries; ours should be too.

> "Hoeveel magnesium heb je echt nodig?" · "Wanneer op de dag werkt
> creatine?" · "Waarom je vitamine D-test niets zegt zonder K2" ·
> "Slaap: wat werkt volgens de studies en wat niet"

Format: 6–10 min YouTube, cut into 3–5 shorts. Target: **weekly, without
exception, for 18 months before judging it.** Views will be embarrassing for
a year. Upfront's were 8.800. Post anyway.

**Line 2 — The audit (from month 4).**
Take one shelf per video. Read every label. Show the arithmetic.

> "Ik heb 40 magnesiumsupplementen gekocht en de doseringen nagerekend" ·
> "Wat 'klinisch bewezen' juridisch betekent" · "De collageen-industrie
> heeft één studie en die is betaald door de fabrikant"

Rule, non-negotiable: **attack the claim, never the person.** Every audit
cites its sources on screen. This is the line that will get us noticed and
also the line that can get us sued — the citations are the defence.

**Line 3 — Radical transparency (from month 9).**
Our COGS, our margins, our failures, our null results.

> "Wat een zoutlamp ons kost" · "Dit product hebben we geschrapt en dit is
> waarom" · "De studie waar wij op hoopten vond niks" · "Wat wij fout hadden
> in artikel #3"

**Line 4 — Human and spectacle (from year 2).**
Upfront's single most-watched video is a man breaking his foot at 100km. Not a
product. Their Opa Wim serial pulled 187K–536K per post. Find the equivalent:
a real person, a real attempt, a number that goes up, filmed daily.

### Cadence, by year

| Year | YouTube | Shorts/Reels/TikTok | Articles | Newsletter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | 1/week | 5/week | 2/month | Weekly (Friday) |
| 2027 | 2/week | 10/week | 3/month | Weekly |
| 2028+ | 3/week + serials | 15/week | 4/month | Weekly + segmented |

The newsletter is the only channel we own. Everything else is rented from a
platform that can change its mind. Treat the list as the asset.

5. Logos for Liam Kratos and Krealio. The Liam Kratos header still carries the

6. Krealio: sizes shop, domains, Vercel. Ship it, then leave it.

**Gate: do not start Phase 1 until a stranger can buy something without help.**

### Phase 1 — Audience before revenue (2026). Target: €250K.

The temptation is to spend this phase optimising a shop nobody visits. Don't.

- Content Line 1 live, weekly, from week one. Line 2 from month four.
- **Merge the Instagram accounts** or commit to one as primary. Two accounts at
  half strength beat neither. Upfront ran one account to 341K.
- Publish 2 articles/month. We have 3 total; we need ~30 by end of 2027 for the
  research moat to be a moat rather than a claim.
- Kratos Weekly every Friday, no exceptions. Build to 5.000 subscribers.
- Product range: 3–5 SKUs. Only things the research already carries.
- **Metric that matters: email subscribers and watch-time.** Not revenue.
  Revenue in 2026 is a rounding error and optimising it costs the decade.

**Gate: 25K YouTube subs or 50K Instagram, and 5K email, before Phase 2.**

### Phase 2 — Product-market fit (2027). Target: €2,5M.

- Range to 15–20 SKUs. Every one carries a published analysis.
- Coaching capped and priced up — it is proof, not scale.
- Guides live and selling.
- Line 3 (transparency) launches. Publish the first COGS breakdown.
- First paid spend, and only now: 6–9% of revenue, mirroring Upfront. Retarget
  people the content already reached. Never top-of-funnel — that is what the
  content is for.
- **Metric: repeat purchase rate.** Below 30% at twelve months, the product is
  wrong and no amount of reach fixes it.

**Gate: 30% repeat rate and €200K/month run-rate before approaching retail.**

### Phase 3 — Retail and Belgium (2028). Target: €12M.

- Approach Albert Heijn / Jumbo / Holland & Barrett with the audience as the
  pitch. Retail buys demand, not products. The follower count is the argument.
- Belgium: same language, same logistics, no new content engine needed.
- Hire: first video producer. In-house studio, as Upfront did — content volume
  is the constraint from here on.
- Category step one: from devices/supplements into **daily consumables**. This
  is where recurring revenue starts and where the €1B path opens.

**Gate: a national retail listing before DACH spend.**

### Phase 4 — DACH and scale (2029–2030). Target: €45M → €120M.

- Germany. Ten times the market and the same regulatory frame. This is the
  single biggest revenue lever in the plan.
- Full German content engine — dubbed does not work, native does. Budget a
  local presenter, not a translation.
- Own production. Upfront's factory cost €250K, was built in six weeks and paid
  back in months at ~€2/bag. Vertical integration is a margin and speed play,
  and it becomes content.
- Category step two: **food.** Not supplements with a food label — real
  groceries, held to the same sourcing standard.

**Gate: German revenue > 25% of total before UK.**

### Phase 5 — Food, at scale (2031–2035). Target: €350M → €1B.

- Food is the majority of revenue by 2033. Supplements become the credibility
  halo that sells the groceries, not the other way round.
- UK + Nordics.
- Own retail, if it earns its place. Upfront's first store did €150K/week —
  a store is a content set that pays for itself.
- The endgame from the manifest — teaching this in schools — becomes a real
  programme with a budget, not an aspiration. At €1B it is affordable, and it
  is the only reason the €1B was worth having.

## 5. The research moat

The thing nobody can copy quickly, and the reason this is not just Upfront with
different products.

- **Every claim traceable, including the null results.** Already the standard on
  the site. Never relax it, especially when a product would sell better without.
- **Publish the full analyses free, forever.** No paywall, no email gate. The
  manifest says knowledge behind a paywall is not a solution; the marketing
  plan must not quietly contradict it.
- **Publish our own failures.** When an article is wrong, the correction is its
  own piece of content, not a silent edit.
- **The datasets stay downloadable.** Nobody else in this category hands over
  the spreadsheet.

Target: **200+ published analyses by 2030.** At 4/month from 2028 that is
reachable. This is what a competitor with more money still cannot buy, because
it takes years and it takes being right.

## 6. What we do not do

Cheaper to write down now than to argue about later.

- **No influencer seeding.** Paying someone to say it works undoes the entire
  proposition in one post.
- **No claims the research does not carry**, even when a competitor is making
  them and winning.
- **No "sustainable" as a word.** Upfront refuse it deliberately: it raises
  questions nobody can answer and cheapens the claims that are real.
- **No discount ladder.** One or two events a year maximum. Permanent discounting
  teaches people the price is fake, which is the same lesson our whole brand
  argues against.
- **No third brand's content engine.** See §2.
- **No competitor named as an enemy.** The manifest names Upfront and Clearly as
  allies and commits to keep doing it. Line 2 audits *claims and categories*,
  never a named company we have called a fellow traveller.

## 9. Risks

- **Three brands, one person.** The most likely failure mode, by a distance. §2 exists for this.
- **18 months of no visible traction.** Upfront's early videos did 8.800 views. The plan requires posting weekly through that silence. Most people stop at month five.
- **Regulatory.** EFSA health claims are strict. Every claim already traces to a source, which is the best possible position, but Line 2 needs a legal read before it scales.
- **Being right is not a growth strategy on its own.** The research moat is a retention and trust asset. Reach still has to be earned separately, every week, on someone else's platform.
- **€1B may simply not happen.** €120M by 2030 would already be a serious company doing the thing the manifest describes. The plan should be run so that stopping at any gate is still a win, not a failure.
